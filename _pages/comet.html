How it works

CoMET starts with a single successful demonstration. Multi-view images, waypoint poses, gripper state, and held-object information describe the task and its expected intermediate states. A generator–reviewer pipeline turns this description into a behaviour tree with conditions for each waypoint.

  1. Define expected conditions. A reusable catalogue supplies task-specific preconditions, hold conditions, and postconditions: what must be true before, during, and after an action.
  2. Monitor physical execution. Lightweight detectors check collision and grasp-loss signals during motion, and position and orientation at waypoint completion. Collision and grasp-loss candidates receive selective visual confirmation.
  3. Verify the task state. At checkpoints, a vision–language model checks the active semantic conditions against side, front, and wrist camera views. This can reveal unmet requirements even when the motion itself appears normal.
  4. Explain deviations. The active tree stage, violated conditions, and supporting evidence help localise a failure. Mapping rules assign benchmark failure categories; detecting a violation does not always reveal its initiating cause.

Evaluation

The study evaluates 78 simulated manipulation tasks adapted from AHA/RLBench with a Franka Panda arm. Seven failure types cover grasp loss, collision, position and orientation deviations, incorrect object selection, omitted grasps, and sequence violations. CoMET requires no failure-specific vision–language model fine-tuning.

Visual checks and robot-state signals provide complementary evidence. In the component ablations, condition verification achieves 43.5% binary F1 and detector monitoring achieves 75.4%, compared with 83.3% for CoMET. Its binary F1 is close to the strongest augmented baseline. Exact failure diagnosis remains harder than detecting that a required condition has been violated.

Current scope and next steps

The evaluation uses procedurally injected failures in simulation. Collision diagnosis and sequence violations remain challenging, and visual confirmation adds inference cost and latency. Physical-robot validation, stronger temporal reasoning, and closed-loop recovery are future work. The stage and violated condition provide a starting point for deciding what needs to be restored before execution continues.
